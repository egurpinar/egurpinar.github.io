One of the changelles of high power density electroics systems is the volume occupied by the auxilary circuitry that does not actively contribute to power processing, such as gate driver circuitry for power transistors. During my time at University of Nottingham, I have worked with the researchers from University of Oviedo, Spain to develop self-powering isolated gate driver circuits for SiC MOSFETs that combine isolated signal and power transfer. The results of this research was published as a [journal paper](/files/Self-Powering_High_Frequency_Modulated_SiC_Power_MOSFET_Isolated_Gate_Driver.pdf) in IEEE Transactions on Industry Applications in 2021. You can find the abstract below: 

"A novel implementation of an isolated gate driver for power switches is proposed in this study. The driver is bespoke designed for
    SiC power MOSFETs. The proposal achieves the main driver characteristics—isolation capability, gate switching command, and power transfer to the secondary side by using a unique magnetic transformer. The resulting simple circuitry enables the integration of the driver into the power cell, achieving theoretical higher power density values of the final system. The principle of operation of the driver is described in detail. The original pulsewidth modulation signal is modulated with a pure ac square waveform under a two-level modulation scheme, that guarantees no satura- tion of the magnetic core. The signal is then magnetically coupled to the secondary side, where it is rectified and reconstructed to ef- fectively drive the target device. In addition, the steps needed to provide a suitable design taking into account the parameters of the target power switch are also detailed. The resulting driver is characterized and a prototype of the driver is built and tested. The main results of the driver performance on a SiC MOSFET-based prototype are presented. Based on the analysis of these results, this study experimentally demonstrates the feasibility of the proposal.
